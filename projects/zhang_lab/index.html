Bacterial Antiviral Defense Systems

Investigating bacterial defense proteins and L7-protein domain evolution at the Broad Institute

Overview

At the Feng Zhang Lab at the Broad Institute of MIT & Harvard, I conduct research on bacterial antiviral defense systems, combining wet-lab techniques with computational approaches to understand protein function and evolution.

Project 1: Bacterial Antiviral Defense System Proteins

Investigated bacterial antiviral defense system proteins using:

  • Bacterial growth assays
  • PCR amplification
  • Molecular cloning techniques

This work helps elucidate how bacteria protect themselves from viral infections, with potential applications in biotechnology and therapeutic development.

Project 2: DRT Reverse Transcriptase System

Conducted comprehensive computational and wet-lab mutagenesis studies of the DRT reverse transcriptase bacterial system:

  • Used bioinformatics tools including Geneious and Benchling
  • Identified essential sequence regions critical for system function
  • Performed targeted mutagenesis to validate computational predictions

Project 3: L7-Protein Domain Evolution (SuperUROP)

Led an independent computational study characterizing the evolutionary diversity of the L7-protein domain:

  • Utilized FoldSeek for structural similarity searches
  • Applied DALI for protein structure comparison
  • Leveraged AlphaFold for structure prediction
  • Created phylogenetic visualizations with iTOL
  • Performed embedding visualization to understand protein space

Recognition: MIT EECS SuperUROP Scholar (1 of 8 computational biology scholars)

This work was presented at the MIT EECS SuperUROP Poster Symposium in 2025.
